About Me

I like flattery, even if it comes from an A.I. machine. I punched my name into Perplexity. Here’s what it said:

“Curtis D. Hardin is a notable figure in the field of social psychology, with a focus on the interpersonal foundations of cognition. His research encompasses a broad range of topics including the self-concept, social identification, prejudice, and ideology. Hardin’s work is particularly focused on how social interactions and relationships influence cognitive processes. One of the key areas of Hardin’s research is shared reality theory, which explores how shared experiences among people can create a sense of objective reality. This theory is relevant to understanding phenomena such as social influence, group cohesion, and the maintenance of social norms. Hardin has also contributed to the study of automatic prejudice and stereotyping, investigating how unconscious biases can be triggered by certain primes and social situations. His work in this area has implications for understanding the persistence of prejudice and discrimination in society. Throughout his career, Hardin has been cited extensively, indicating the impact of his research on the field of social psychology. His work has been influential in shaping current understanding of how interpersonal dynamics affect cognitive processes and social behavior. In summary, Curtis D. Hardin’s research has significantly contributed to the understanding of how social contexts influence cognition, particularly in the areas of self-concept, social identification, prejudice, and ideology. His work on shared reality theory and automatic stereotyping has been particularly influential in the field of social psychology.”

This is accurate enough, minus the puffery regarding “influence.” However, it leaves out about half of shared reality theory. In addition to the role of social verification in epistemic realization, shared reality regulates interpersonal relationships. To know something is to connect to someone (Hardin & Conley, 2001; Hardin & Higgins, 1996).
